- (direction by user) Product principle: teach only what is genuinely useful.
- (decision by user) Lexickon combines vocabulary utility scoring with flashcards and spaced repetition; the differentiating moment is deciding what deserves study before memorization.
- (direction by user) Initial audience: intermediate English learners who are software developers or other IT professionals reading English work materials; first professional domain is development/programming.
- (decision by user) V1 is native iOS: add words and phrases, score individual words by general and development-domain utility and part of speech, create cards, and review with spaced repetition; phrase utility scoring is excluded.
- (decision by user) V1 excludes Android, additional professional domains, smart card construction, and voice AI analysis.
- (direction by user) Validate first as a personal product experiment, then publish on iOS to test broader demand and commercial potential.
- (assumption by user) Primary risk: the broader audience may not experience word selection as an important problem or may resist changing its flashcard workflow.
- (direction by user) Long-term possibilities: more professional domains, smart card creation, and an AI voice coach that identifies repeated errors and creates personalized decks.
- (gap by coach) Current success criteria are directional but not yet operationally measurable; scoring trust and the user's current selection workaround remain unexamined.
- (gap by coach) Repo conflict: current iOS MVP plan ends at frequency search and has no card/deck/SRS stage, while the proposed brief puts card creation and spaced repetition in V1; product truth must be chosen before drafting.
- (question by coach) V1 usefulness behavior is unresolved: show general and programming scores side-by-side, combine them, or let the user switch one active domain.
- (gap by coach) Usefulness metric, parts-of-speech contract, and phrase semantics are not yet settled in current product/technical plans.
- (assumption by coach) Planned auth, dataset selection, and pack installation may add first-use friction; the brief should acknowledge onboarding only if it materially affects validation.
- (insight by coach) Landscape: WordUp already prioritizes useful words and Engineering English ranks curated software terms, so broad claims that existing tools do not help choose words are unsafe.
- (direction by coach) Defensible wedge to test: an explainable side-by-side general-English versus software-development usefulness score for an arbitrary encountered word at the moment before adding it to study.
- (insight by coach) Frequency supports prioritization but is not equivalent to usefulness; the scoring model must define corpus, domain, unit of analysis, thresholds, and explanation.
- (risk by coach) Excluding phrase scoring may weaken the software-development wedge because many high-value technical units are multi-word terms.
- (question by coach) Does Lexickon evaluate a word the user encountered, proactively recommend words, or both? This determines the job-to-be-done and differentiation.
- (gap by coach) Personal validation needs an explicit duration, baseline, observation method, metrics, and publish/rethink threshold; public validation needs demand evidence without assuming analytics infrastructure.
- (question by coach) Long-term voice AI may be too disconnected from the initial word-selection thesis for a 1–2 page brief and may belong in the addendum unless the connecting strategy is explicit.
