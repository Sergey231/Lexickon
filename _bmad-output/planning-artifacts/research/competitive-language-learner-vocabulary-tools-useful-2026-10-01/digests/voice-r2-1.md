# voice-r2-1: user voice on choosing which words to save (Anki, LingQ, Migaku, Readlang, Lingvist, Clozemaster)

Method note: Reddit is blocked to the fetch tool and the domain filter, and App Store/Trustpilot pages were not directly fetched. Most evidence is therefore forum posts plus aggregator pages (justuseapp, search summaries). Distinct voices are few; treat as thin. Search-engine summaries are aggregators and are not used as sole support for any claim.

## Findings

1. claim: Anki forum user (Dec 2023) found a batch of 10 new cards too many to retain, and asked for ~5. Quote: "This amount is too big for me to remember". One voice. Concerns batch size, not word selection.
   - source: https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/too-many-learning-cards-at-once/38017
   - publisher: Anki forums (community) | pub_date: 2023-12-04 | accessed=2026-10-01 | confidence: med | class: user-voice, complaint (volume), older than 12 months

2. claim: A Bunpro community thread on sentence mining (summer, year not confirmed by fetch) has users describing "i+1" filtering of decks and calling mining "a huge time sink" in Anki. One user moved to a green/yellow/red word-status system instead of cards. Two to three voices; indirect evidence of selection/bloat effort.
   - source: https://community.bunpro.jp/t/whats-your-experience-with-sentence-mining/49802
   - publisher: Bunpro community | pub_date: unknown (July, year unverified) | accessed=2026-10-01 | confidence: low | class: user-voice, mixed

3. claim: Lingvist low-star app-store reviews (aggregated by justuseapp) say that after about 3,000 words "don't see a new word for months", with review backlog burnout after breaks. No complaint found about irrelevant words; the complaint is the opposite (too little new vocabulary and no user control). Multiple reviewers per the page, but exact count and dates were not shown, so a distinct-voice count is not established. Another reviewer says sentence selection is business-focused with no thematic variety (a weak domain-vocabulary signal, 1 voice).
   - source: https://justuseapp.com/en/app/969093402/lingvist-learn-languages/reviews
   - publisher: justuseapp (aggregator of App Store reviews) | pub_date: undated | accessed=2026-10-01 | confidence: low | class: user-voice via aggregator, complaint

4. claim: Clozemaster forum users treat its frequency-based vocabulary as a baseline, not a complete tool: "mastering everything on a Clozemaster language definitely won't make you speak like a native". They supplement with e-reader dictionaries and their own highlighted words. This is mild praise plus a gap, with 1-2 voices. Frequency ordering is the product's selection mechanism, so users do not choose.
   - source: https://forum.clozemaster.com/t/clozemaster-vocabulary-vs-harry-potter/10039
   - publisher: Clozemaster forum | pub_date: unknown (thread id suggests 2019-2020) | accessed=2026-10-01 | confidence: low | class: user-voice, praise/gap, likely older than 12 months

5. claim: Readlang forum request (Dec, year unverified) asks that every word in a text show multiple contextual examples. This is about context, not selection or usefulness. Readlang's own docs say it prioritizes more frequent words in practice and offers a "Keep Flashcard" setting on deselect (from a search summary, not verified on the primary page).
   - source: https://forum.readlang.com/t/unknown-words-in-one-file/4714
   - publisher: Readlang forum | pub_date: unknown | accessed=2026-10-01 | confidence: low | class: user-voice, feature request (tangential)

6. claim: Migaku community members report words marked "known" because they appeared in mined sentences although they were not the targets. Advice is to mine only single-unknown ("i+1") sentences, which Migaku surfaces automatically. The user had mined about 300 cards. This is a selection-hygiene complaint, not a usefulness complaint. Found only through a search summary; the page fetch returned 410, so the quote is unverified.
   - source: https://migaku.com/community/m/1313815677307719760 (410 on fetch)
   - publisher: Migaku community | pub_date: unknown | accessed=2026-10-01 | confidence: low | class: user-voice, complaint, unverified

7. claim: LingQ itself recommends 10 to 15% unknown words as ideal and shows the unknown-word percentage per lesson, with high values in red. The product pushes learners to choose content by difficulty rather than by word usefulness. This is vendor guidance, not user voice, and comes via search summary of lingq.com/FluentU/Lingopie pages.
   - source: https://www.fluentu.com/blog/lingq-review/ (and lingq.com course page)
   - publisher: FluentU (secondary) | pub_date: unknown | accessed=2026-10-01 | confidence: low | class: vendor/review-site, not user voice

## Overall read (limited evidence)

- Seen: overload of new cards (Anki, 1 voice), review backlog/burnout (Lingvist), hygiene of what counts as known (Migaku), effort of curating (Bunpro thread).
- Not seen: any user explicitly asking for a per-word usefulness, frequency or priority rating before saving, or for per-domain vocabulary, in a source I could read. Absence here reflects access limits and is not proof the demand does not exist.
- Voice count of verifiable, readable, user-originated items from within 12 months: 0 confirmed (dates mostly missing or older). Do not rely on this digest for recency.
